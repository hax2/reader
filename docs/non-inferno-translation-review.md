## Source and reader limitations

Some source transcriptions contain garbled words, missing text or licensing boilerplate. Where an obvious transcription error was clarified from the published source, the lookup key was retained; the English may express the clarified reading. In *Nené traviesa*, for example, the supplied “edad” in block 22 corresponds to “mitad” in [a published edition](https://dgb.cultura.gob.mx/Documentos/PublicacionesDGB/BibliotecaInfantil/JoseMarti.pdf). *Los rizos* contains corrupted electronic-edition boilerplate, and its narrative was checked against [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/los-rizos--0/html/ffb5261e-82b1-11df-acc7-002185ce6064_2.html). The *Cuentos de amor* transcript also includes badly corrupted licensing and trailing boilerplate. *La carta robada* has source anomalies in blocks 55 and 69; the saved Spanish was retained.

The reader uses exact saved keys first and can infer sentence translations from paragraph translations. Exact saved-key lookup was tested, but inferred sentence segmentation is heuristic and was not independently validated for every sentence.

The Google and local catalog translation scripts require `--replace-existing` before replacing an existing incomplete or stale saved file, including one with manual corrections.
